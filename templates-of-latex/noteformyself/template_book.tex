\begin{proof}[Proof sketch for \cref{mainthm:book}]
  The proof can be divided into referenceable steps and cases.

  \begin{step}[Set up the comparison]\label{step:book-setup}
    Choose the canonical maps supplied by the two universal properties.
  \end{step}

  \begin{claim}[
    title={The composites are identities},
    label=\labelNFM{claim:book}
  ]
    Both composites agree with the corresponding identity morphisms.
  \end{claim}

  \begin{case}[The source is initial]\label{case:book-initial}
    Uniqueness of a map from the source proves the first identity.
  \end{case}

  \begin{case}[The target is terminal]\label{case:book-terminal}
    Uniqueness of a map to the target proves the second identity.
  \end{case}

  Thus \cref{step:book-setup}, \cref{claim:book}, and
  \cref{case:book-initial} complete the argument.
\end{proof}

An aligned equation can carry an ordinary label:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:base-change}
  Lg^* Rf_*\mathcal{F} \simeq Rf'_* Lg'^*\mathcal{F}.
\end{equation}
The reference \cref{eq:base-change} uses the standard cleveref behavior.

\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
  X' \arrow[r,"g'"] \arrow[d,"f'"'] & X \arrow[d,"f"] \\
  Y' \arrow[r,"g"'] & Y
\end{tikzcd}
\]

Nested lists use alphabetic and roman labels:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Verify existence.
  \item Verify uniqueness.
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item compare the two candidates;
      \item apply the universal property.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

\begin{lstlisting}[
  language=Python,
  caption={A small source-code listing},

def compose(f, g):
    """Return the composite g after f."""
    return lambda x: g(f(x))
\end{lstlisting}
